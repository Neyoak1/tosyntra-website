Most outbound reports answer the easiest question, how much did we send, and skip the useful one: what produced pipeline, and why?

Here is the measurement framework we use. It is designed to tell a team where to spend its next hour, not just to fill a dashboard.

## Stop leaning on open rates

Open rates were never precise, and they have become less reliable. Privacy features in some email apps load images automatically, which can register an open nobody made, while some setups block tracking entirely. Many teams also turn tracking off to protect deliverability.

Treat opens as a rough health check at most. Make decisions on replies, meetings and pipeline.

## The metrics, in the order they happen

### 1. Deliverability

- **Bounce rate.** The share of emails that could not be delivered. A high rate means your list is not being verified properly, and it damages your sending reputation quickly.
- **Spam complaint rate.** Watch it in Google Postmaster Tools. Google advises staying below 0.3%, and much lower is healthier.
- **Inbox placement.** Periodic seed tests show whether you are landing in the inbox or in spam.

If deliverability is broken, nothing below it means anything.

### 2. Replies

- **Reply rate.** All replies divided by people contacted.
- **Positive reply rate.** Replies showing interest, divided by people contacted. This is the number that matters.

Count by **person contacted**, not by email sent. A five-step sequence to 100 people is 100 prospects, not 500 attempts.

Classify every reply: interested, not now, wrong person, not interested, unsubscribe, out of office. AI can do a first pass well if a person spot-checks it.

### 3. Meetings

- **Meetings booked** per 100 people contacted
- **Meeting show rate**
- **Qualified meetings**: meetings that met your criteria afterwards

### 4. Pipeline and revenue

- **Pipeline created** from outbound-sourced deals
- **Win rate** and **sales cycle length** for outbound deals compared with other sources
- **Cost per meeting** and **cost per opportunity**, including data, tools and time

## Cut every metric the right way

A single overall reply rate hides almost everything. Break results down by:

- **Signal**, such as hiring, funding or a leadership change. This tells you which triggers are worth watching.
- **Segment**: industry, company size, region.
- **Persona**: the title or role contacted.
- **Message**: which version of the opening line or offer was used.
- **Mailbox and domain**, to catch deliverability problems early.

The goal is a table where you can see, for example, that companies hiring their first sales leader reply at three times the rate of everything else, and then put more effort there.

## Make the numbers trustworthy

Reporting fails more often from bad data than from bad charts.

1. **Define every metric in writing.** What counts as a positive reply? When is a meeting qualified? Put the definitions where the whole team can see them.
2. **Keep one source of truth.** Every report should read from the same table or CRM object, not from separate exports that drift apart.
3. **Tag at the point of sending.** Record the signal, segment and message version on each prospect when they enter a campaign. You cannot reconstruct it reliably afterwards.
4. **Check data quality weekly.** Look for duplicates, missing owners, unclassified replies and deals with no source.

## A simple weekly review

Once a week, answer four questions:

1. Is deliverability healthy across every mailbox and domain?
2. Which signal and segment produced the most positive replies per 100 prospects?
3. Which message version won, and is the difference big enough to trust?
4. What is one change we will test next week?

Write the answer to the fourth question down, with the date. A short log of experiments becomes one of the most valuable records a sales team has.
