GTM engineering grew up in American and European software companies. The playbooks, tools and job posts mostly assume a buyer who lives in their inbox, a CRM full of clean data and a budget for several paid data providers.

Selling in Nigeria, and across much of Africa, rarely looks like that. That does not make GTM engineering less useful here. It makes it more useful, as long as the systems are designed for how people here actually buy.

## What is different about selling in African markets

**Buyers live on WhatsApp.** For many Nigerian businesses, WhatsApp is the main sales channel. Enquiries arrive there, quotes are sent there and deals are closed there. A system that only tracks email misses most of the pipeline.

**Phone calls still matter.** In lending, insurance, education, real estate and many B2B services, a call is how trust is built and how details get verified.

**Data is thinner.** The big contact databases cover African companies unevenly. Many good businesses have a thin web presence, so enrichment needs more sources, more checking and sometimes local knowledge.

**Budgets are tighter and in naira.** Tools priced in dollars per seat or per credit add up quickly. Systems have to earn their cost, and cheaper building blocks such as n8n, Google Sheets and Supabase often make more sense than an expensive all-in-one stack.

**Relationships carry weight.** Referrals, associations and personal introductions remain powerful. Automation should support that, not replace it with volume.

## What GTM engineering looks like here

The principles are the same anywhere: know who to sell to, notice when they are ready, reach them with a reason, and measure what works. The build changes.

- **WhatsApp-first lead capture.** Every enquiry captured as a lead, followed up consistently and tracked to a sale. We built exactly this for [Nigerian private schools](/work/noventis-schools), where parents contact admissions on WhatsApp first.
- **Voice agents for verification and follow-up.** An [AI call agent](/work/ai-outbound-call-agent) can verify customer details, send reminders and log every conversation, which is useful for fintechs and lenders handling thousands of customers.
- **Blended enrichment.** Combining paid data with public registries, company websites, LinkedIn and AI-assisted research, then verifying before anything is used.
- **Cost-aware design.** Tracking cost per enriched account and per meeting, and choosing tools to match.

## The opportunity for African GTM engineers

There is also an opportunity in the other direction. International companies need GTM engineering help, and a growing number are comfortable working with remote specialists. African GTM engineers who can show real, documented systems are well placed to win that work.

What earns trust is the same everywhere:

1. **Public proof of work.** Workflows on GitHub, walkthrough videos and honest case studies that separate demos from client results.
2. **Clear communication.** Written documentation, predictable updates and a few hours of overlap with the client's working day.
3. **Practical arrangements.** Being clear about contracts, invoicing and payment options before work begins.

## How to start if you run a Nigerian or African sales team

Start small and measurable:

1. Write down how a lead reaches your team today, from first contact to payment.
2. Find the step where most leads are lost or delayed. It is often the first response.
3. Automate that one step and measure it for a month.
4. Only then add enrichment, signals or AI research.
